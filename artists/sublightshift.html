SubLight Shift

Juno and Vex Ardin live in New Babbage. Not on stage. Not in clubs. In technical corridors, between server racks, cooling systems, and maintenance lines. They are IT maintenance technicians. On microTech, that kind of profile does not surprise anyone.

Music was never a profession. Just a playground. They listen to everything, mostly electronic music. If they can mix it, even better. They tinker, test, and assemble sounds the same way they assemble systems. Without a plan. Without public ambition. A few mixtapes circulate under different pseudonyms, lost in the depths of Spectrum, in sections dedicated to New Babbage. You have to look for them, and above all, know what you are looking for.

Sometimes, they play at Wally’s Bar. Never announced. Never identified. Always masked. Not to create mystery, but to stay at ease. They want to be able to go home afterward, return to work the next day, as if nothing had happened. That is where their pleasure lies. Creating. Testing. Watching heads nod, without anyone really knowing why.

Nok noticed them there. Not right away. He heard the tracks first, then the same signatures appearing again. The same balance. The same way of letting the drops breathe. And that tiny detail. A finger snap, always at the same moment. Vex does not even notice it anymore. Nok did.

It took time. A real investigation. Until the day he found them during a maintenance operation on the Omega Pro sales terminals. Nok had just bought a new generator there for his new ship, the one he had been searching for for months to serve as his mobile studio. He was missing just a bit of electrical power to run everything. In the back of the store, an electronic track was playing. Juno and Vex were working. Their heads swayed slightly. And the finger snap came back.

Surprised to have been uncovered, they listened. Nok did not talk about a career. He talked about a project. Other people like them. Improvised voices. Music made without calculation. They already knew The People’s Radio. They listened regularly to shows like Titan Techno or Deep Bass Mission. They knew what that meant.

The decision took eight seconds. A look. A silence. A yes spoken in perfect stereo, with Nok standing exactly in the middle. Nothing guaranteed the project would come together. But refusing might have meant giving up something they would never be able to rebuild on their own.

Autumn arrived. After a difficult maintenance operation on the spaceport elevator management servers, a message appeared on their mobiGlass.

October 13 to 19. Meet in Levski.

SubLight Shift is not a band. It is a movement. A controlled drift. Juno and Vex do not play to be seen. They synchronize. They test. They still enjoy themselves. And in that serious game between machines, signals, and silences, they agreed to leave a trace.

Just one.
