Brick was fifteen when his father was killed during a Vanduul attack on the mining outpost where he worked.

What remained were not grand final words, but ordinary traces: messages, work transmissions, jokes, small pieces of a life that had never expected to become memories.

Brick kept writing.

He wrote about people more easily than he wrote about himself. Workers, absences, tired faces, small acts of loyalty. Things people rarely notice until they are gone.

On stage, he never becomes louder than necessary. He is not aggressive or theatrical. He simply seems to know exactly where to stand, how much space a line needs, and when silence should be left alone.

That same restraint follows him away from the microphone. Brick has little interest in explaining himself through interviews or promotion. He would rather let a song remain open enough for someone else to find their own story inside it.

For Brick, Levski is not an idea to defend from a distance. It is home. He knows its faults, its compromises, and the people who keep it functioning anyway. His writing carries the same quiet belief he grew up around: importance is measured less by what someone claims than by what remains when something breaks.

He first heard about SubOrbital Records through The People's Radio.

There was no chance encounter and no elaborate invitation. Brick contacted the station, sent over a few recordings and texts, and eventually they reached Nok Varen.

That was enough.

Over time, Brick began recognizing how much his father had taught him without ever calling it teaching: judge people by what they do, learn before speaking, notice who stays when something breaks.

*Transmission* grew from that realization — an imagined conversation with his younger self, passing forward what took years to understand.

*Already Free* turns the same attention toward Levski. Not as a symbol of rebellion, but as a place that learned long ago to survive without waiting for permission.

Brick does not write manifestos.

He writes about people who keep things standing.
